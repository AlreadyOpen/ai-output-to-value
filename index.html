Open, evidence-led guidance for business decisions

From AI output to business value.

A free or inexpensive AI agent can generate remarkably complete work. That does not automatically establish a client-ready deliverable, repeatable organisational capability, or valuable business outcome.

Use AI ambitiously. Keep the claims clear.

Start here The frame is already done?

Four distinctions worth remembering

Access is not capability. A licence—or a free agent—provides access to capability, not proof that the organisation can use it effectively for a particular job.
Output is not completion. Generation may be only one part of specification, review, integration, delivery, and support.
Polish is not proof of substance. A finished-looking artefact may combine verified requirements with plausible assumptions.
Activity is not value. More prompts, tokens, generated code, licences, meetings, or travel are not outcomes by themselves.

This project's decision framework

Six different claims a business may need to establish.

The arrows are a memory aid, not six mandatory steps. A disposable prototype can create a valuable learning outcome without ever becoming an operational service.

  1. 01

    Access

    We have a model, API, subscription, agent, or tool.

    Does not prove effective use.
  2. 02

    Output

    The system produced an artefact.

    Does not prove correctness or client fit.
  3. 03

    Deliverable

    The result is fit for a defined intended use.

    Does not prove repeatability.
  4. 04

    Capability

    The organisation can verify, operate, support, maintain, and improve it.

    Does not prove a valuable outcome.
  5. 05

    Outcome

    Something meaningful changed.

    Does not prove the gain exceeds cost.
  6. 06

    Value

    The outcome is worth the full cost, risk, and trade-offs.

    Does not imply every task needs the same route.

Reverse planning: What outcome do we need, and what evidence, capability, and work would make that outcome plausible?

The substance gap

A capable agent is not automatically a capable supplier.

A simple example: a contact form can look complete while enquiries never reach the business. The interface exists; the promised workflow does not.

Capability 1

Tool capability

Can the agent generate the website, application, report, workflow, integration, or deployment?

Capability 2

Job substance

Does the result reflect the actual requirements, business rules, environment, integrations, constraints, evidence, and relevant risks?

Capability 3

Delivery capability

Can the supplier verify, explain, deploy, support, maintain, correct, and stand behind the result?

Do not mix the terms

They describe different dimensions, and they can overlap.

Way of working

Vibe coding

Prompt-driven software creation and rapid iteration, sometimes with limited inspection of the generated implementation.

AI involvement

AI-assisted work

AI contributed to producing or performing the work. The term alone says nothing about review, controls, or quality.

Practice

Responsible AI-assisted practice

The workflow includes proportionate evaluation, ownership, and controls for the intended use.

Quality problem

AI slop / workslop

Output that appears finished but lacks enough substance, correctness, context, or usefulness for the person who must use it.

A vibe-coded artefact is AI-assisted. Whether it is useful, responsible for the intended use, or low-value output is a separate assessment.

Use this in a meeting

Seven questions before the next decision.

  1. 1

    What exactly have we demonstrated?
    Separate a working demo from assumptions about readiness or demand.

  2. 2

    What did the AI know, and what did it infer?
    Make generated assumptions visible.

  3. 3

    What remains before the intended use?
    Name unresolved work and decisions.

  4. 4

    Which work disappeared, and which work moved elsewhere?
    Look beyond generation time.

  5. 5

    Who owns verification, approval, operation, and support?
    Make handoffs explicit.

  6. 6

    Which business outcome are we trying to change?
    Name the measure before celebrating the activity.

  7. 7

    What evidence would justify the next decision?
    Decide what would change the answer.

Evidence, not logo collecting

Important claims should be traceable to what the evidence actually supports.

The publication separates source records from claim records. Claims include locators, qualifications, review status, and—in product-capability cases—availability status where relevant.

Industry research DORA — State of AI-assisted Software Development 2025

Evidence on AI adoption within wider software-delivery and organisational systems.

 Official guidance UK Government — AI Playbook

Lifecycle governance, expertise, meaningful human control, testing, monitoring, and accountability.

 Research study Generative AI at Work

Measured productivity effects in a specific customer-support setting, with heterogeneous effects across workers.

 Claim-level evidence Inspect the evidence

See exact claim text, source locators, qualifications, availability status, and human-review state.

Vendor examples such as Replit remain useful case evidence, but they are not the foundation of the project's central argument.

Beyond the API key — and beyond the free agent

Use AI ambitiously. Establish what you can stand behind.

Prototype quickly. Use inexpensive tools when they are enough. Then match the claim, evidence, assurance, ownership, and economics to the actual intended use.
