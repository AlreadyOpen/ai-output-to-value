# Who stands behind this guide?

**AI Output to Value** is an open publication within **AlreadyOpen**.

The initial maintainer is **Helen Kwok**. The project is intended to help technical and nontechnical decision-makers make more precise claims about AI-assisted work, delivery capability, outcomes, and business value.

It is not presented as an industry standard, certification scheme, consultancy benchmark, or complete operating system for AI transformation.

## What "evidence-led" means here

The project does not treat a prestigious logo or a working link as evidence by itself.

Important factual claims can be traced through the publication evidence system:

**Claim → source/version → exact locator → relevant finding → qualification → publication location → review record → disposition**

The public evidence page shows the current state of that record. Pending review remains visible rather than being silently described as approved.

The project also distinguishes evidence types. A measured study, official guidance, vendor documentation, an internal observation, an illustrative teaching case, and an editorial recommendation do not support the same kinds of claims.

## Independent review is actor-neutral

The project does not assume that review becomes reliable merely because a human performs it.

An independent review may be performed by a human, AI system, automated method, specialist toolchain, or hybrid process if it is sufficiently separate from the originating work, inspects the relevant evidence directly, applies the stated criteria, and leaves an auditable record.

A legal, contractual, professional, or governance rule may still require a named person to approve a decision. That is an authority requirement, not proof that human judgement is inherently more accurate.

## Confidential work stays confidential

A public guide should not require organisations to disclose client names, contracts, production metrics, security details, personal data, or proprietary workflows merely to demonstrate rigour.

If confidential work informs a decision internally, the organisation can keep the full source record private while documenting the claim, method, acceptance rule, and decision for the people authorised to inspect it.

If a public teaching pattern is created from confidential experience, it should be sufficiently abstracted or anonymised to protect the underlying engagement and must not be presented as independently verifiable public evidence.

The fictional cases in this project are labelled **illustrative**. They should not be read as disguised client stories.

## Corrections and challenge

The guide is expected to change when evidence, technology, or the reasoning changes.

Readers can use the public correction route to identify:

- a factual error;
- a source that does not support the published claim;
- a missing qualification or counterexample;
- an accessibility or publication problem;
- a case where the framework leads to an unhelpful decision.

See [Corrections and independent review](corrections.md) and the [Evidence policy](../docs/evidence-policy.md).
